# Cross-cutting Operational Requirements and Practices for Satellite MCS / Ground Segments (as of Oct 2026)

Research notes for the MCS gap analysis. Dates are given so the reader can tell what is current and what is older. "(older)" means the source is from before 2022 but is still widely used as a reference. Tool note: one WebFetch summary of the SpaceOps-2025 EGS-CC paper made up details (hot standby, containers) that the paper does not contain. That summary was discarded, and only the extracted PDF text is cited below.

## What do agencies and operators consider mandatory before flight (operations readiness)?

### Takeaway
The ECSS and Aerospace Corp. baselines agree. Before flight you need: a validated operations database and flight operations procedures (FOP/MOP), checked first against a simulator and then against the real spacecraft (SVTs); a team that is trained and certified per position; end-to-end dataflow and mission readiness tests; rehearsals of nominal and contingency operations (at least two for LEOP, the last within 30 days of launch); operational configuration control with freeze periods; and an anomaly response process (ART/ARB) that is defined in advance. A formal ORR/FRR then confirms that the hardware, software, people, procedures and documentation reflect the deployed state.

### Cited Findings
**Review gates**
- NASA's Operational Readiness Review (ORR) "examines the actual system characteristics and the procedures used in the system or end product's operation and ensures that all system and support (flight and ground) hardware, software, personnel, procedures, and user documentation accurately reflect the deployed state". The FRR then confirms readiness "for a safe and successful flight or launch and for subsequent flight operations". — [NASA NPR 7123.1 App. G](https://nodis3.gsfc.nasa.gov/displayCA.cfm?Internal_ID=N_PR_7123_001A_&page_name=AppendixG)
- ECSS-E-ST-70C (Ground systems and operations, 31 Jul 2008, still the active Level-2 standard) separates ground segment engineering from operations engineering. It aligns process names and reviews with ECSS-E-ST-10C and provides DRDs for all major ground and operations documents. — [ECSS E-70 training presentation, May 2021](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/ECSS-E-70-Training-Presentation-v1-(2021).pdf); [ECSS-E-ST-70C](https://eop-cfi.esa.int/Repo/PUBLIC/DOCUMENTATION/SYSTEM_SUPPORT_DOCS/ECSS%20Standards%20for%20Ground%20Segments/ECSS-E-ST-70C-Ground%20systems.pdf)
- The Level-3 standards under E-70 are: E-70-01 on-board control procedures, E-70-11 space segment operability (Rev.1 dated 15 Oct 2025), E-70-31 monitoring and control data definition, E-70-32 test and operations procedure language (PLUTO), and E-70-41 PUS. — [ECSS active standards](https://ecss.nl/standards/active-standards/engineering/); [ECSS-E-ST-70-32C](https://ecss.nl/standard/ecss-e-st-70-32c-test-and-operations-procedure-language/)

**ECSS E-70 operations engineering process (the pre-flight chain)**
- Requirements and concept phase outputs: Mission Analysis Report, Mission Operations Concept Document (MOCD), operational interface definitions, an Operations Engineering Plan (staffing, team, schedule), and an Operational Validation Plan (tests, resources, pass/fail criteria). — [ECSS E-70 training](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/ECSS-E-70-Training-Presentation-v1-(2021).pdf)
- Data validation happens in two stages. The databases and the MOP are validated "(1) Against a simulator emulating the spacecraft and ground system behaviour … (2) Against the real spacecraft equipment in flight realistic ground based system validation tests". Some parameters can only be validated by inspection or analysis. — [ECSS E-70 training](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/ECSS-E-70-Training-Presentation-v1-(2021).pdf)
- Team build-up: staff are "trained/certified against the mission requirements". Console controllers (SPACONs) "are usually recruited last but need the most formal training/certification". The need for spacecraft-supplier experts "is critical and often underestimated". — [same](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/ECSS-E-70-Training-Presentation-v1-(2021).pdf)
- Operational validation means running the whole system against realistic nominal and contingency scenarios. This covers simulations and rehearsals that validate team structure, the FOP and ground equipment, plus Dataflow and Mission Readiness Tests involving ground station and network staff. The output is a "fully validated Ground segment including teams, procedures and equipment ready for in-orbit operations". — [same](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/ECSS-E-70-Training-Presentation-v1-(2021).pdf)
- Operational configuration management: all configurable parameters go under configuration control (ECSS-M-ST-40), with formal change authorisation and defined freeze periods. In practice this starts during data validation, because ground software deliveries continue in parallel. — [same](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/ECSS-E-70-Training-Presentation-v1-(2021).pdf)
- Execution modes: in critical operations (LEOP, major manoeuvres), operators work under the direct instruction of a team leader (e.g. the SOM) with experts on hand. In routine operations, SPACONs execute directly from the MOP and deviate "only after following the proscribed formal protocols". — [same](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/ECSS-E-70-Training-Presentation-v1-(2021).pdf)

**Aerospace Corp. mission assurance practices for satellite operations (TOR-2013-00293, older but still the main US reference)**
- Before launch: command and telemetry databases with all command arguments, plus multiple limit sets per phase (launch/ascent, transfer orbit, on-orbit). The TM database must also meet trending needs. There should be an operations manual covering nominal and contingency operations, and procedures developed and validated with tooling that imports them from the OM. — [Aerospace TOR-2013-00293](https://aerospace.org/sites/default/files/maiw/TOR-2013-00293.pdf)
- Certification is position-specific. It comes after a training plan and consists of a written test plus an on-console observation. Training records must be kept for each person's whole tenure on the program, along with recurring proficiency training. — [same](https://aerospace.org/sites/default/files/maiw/TOR-2013-00293.pdf)
- Best practice is at least two LEOP exercises or rehearsals, with "the last simulation or rehearsal … held within 30 days of launch". Use the highest-fidelity simulation, run as near real time as possible, and do not announce injected anomalies to the team. — [same](https://aerospace.org/sites/default/files/maiw/TOR-2013-00293.pdf)
- HMI verification should include review by subject matter experts and testing against a simulator. Displays are expected to evolve through interface tests and rehearsals. — [same](https://aerospace.org/sites/default/files/maiw/TOR-2013-00293.pdf)
- Anomaly process: an Anomaly Response Team (ART) investigates, using root-cause tools such as Ishikawa diagrams. An Anomaly Review Board (ARB) dispositions the outcome. Recovery products are simulated and tested at the highest available fidelity, put under configuration control, and trained before execution. The ART assesses "reach-across" to sister satellites. On loss of signal, the first step is to exonerate the ground system. — [same](https://aerospace.org/sites/default/files/maiw/TOR-2013-00293.pdf)
- Shift handover: trend deviations are logged on a watch list and "briefed at crew changeover". Lesson learned: "the absence of alarms does not mean that the other units or subsystems were not affected", so plot all subsystems around the time of an anomaly. — [same](https://aerospace.org/sites/default/files/maiw/TOR-2013-00293.pdf)
- At ESOC, anomalies raised by Space Operations Engineers are tracked in the Anomaly Report Tracking System (ARTS). — [SpaceOps 2025 search result (ID 0152/0480)](https://publications.spaceops.org/2025/download_by_id.php?id=0152). I did not verify this against the full paper.
- NASA runs a small-spacecraft anomaly reporting process (SCAF anomaly alerts, 2023). — [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/10-burkhard-scaf-anomaly-alert-chart-package-20230321-fnl-2.pdf?emrc=8a0639)
- ESA flight control teams run simulation campaigns even while flying, for example ahead of BepiColombo's arrival, to align on go/no-go criteria. — [ESA BepiColombo simulations](https://www.esa.int/Enabling_Support/Operations/Training_while_flying_Inside_BepiColombo_s_arrival_simulations). DLR/GSOC adds gamified anomaly-handling training (board game "Operation Luna") on top of simulator and control-room training. — [SpaceOps 2025 #234](https://publications.spaceops.org/2025/download_by_id.php?id=0234)

**Regulatory readiness gate (EU, emerging)**
- The proposed EU Space Act, Art. 88, requires threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) by accredited assessors before launch, or before the first batch of a constellation, and at least every 3 years after that. — [Mondaq / Hogan Lovells summary](https://www.mondaq.com/cybersecurity/1785852/beyond-nis-2-the-eu-space-act-and-the-coming-of-age-of-space-cybersecurity); [Hogan Lovells, 15 May 2026](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/the-eu-space-act-proposal-in-the-legislative-process-cybersecurity-market-access-and-implications)

### Inferences
- What an MCS has to provide to support ORR is: a versioned, configuration-controlled TM/TC database and procedure set with freeze and baseline support; simulator connectivity, so the same procedures run against the simulator and the real spacecraft; per-position role and certification records, or integration with a training-records system; on-console logs and shift handover notes with watch lists; and anomaly tickets linked to telemetry snapshots. A product that lacks baseline/freeze or procedure validation status will struggle at ORR.
- "Operator certification" is usually enforced procedurally, but an MCS can enforce it through RBAC, for example by allowing only users certified for a position to hold a commanding role.

### Gaps
- I did not find a public, current (2024–2026) ESA ORR/FRR checklist specific to ground segments. The ECSS-E-ST-70C DRD annex is paywall-free but was not parsed here.
- I found no published commercial-operator (NewSpace) ORR template. Commercial practice appears to follow NASA/Aerospace practice informally.

## What are the top cybersecurity controls expected for a commercial satellite ground segment in 2024-2026?

### Takeaway
The baseline today is the following set: NIST CSF profiles (IR 8401 for the ground segment, IR 8270 for commercial operators); CISA SSCIWG recommendations (Apr 2024); and Aerospace Corp's SPARTA v4 ground-segment Defense-in-Depth (8 layers, 78 sub-layers, baseline vs enhanced tiers, Rev A Mar 2026). The highest-priority controls are: MFA everywhere privileged; RBAC/ABAC with just-in-time privilege; two-person approval for high-impact commands, keys and command products; HSM-held keys with CCSDS SDLS (AES-GCM, anti-replay, OTAR) on the link; IT/OT/mission network segmentation and Zero Trust; tamper-resistant centralised audit logs with SIEM/SOC; a command-level intrusion detection system; supply-chain SBOMs; and incident reporting. In the EU, NIS2 already applies to ground infrastructure operators, and the draft EU Space Act would add 12h/24h/72h reporting and TLPT.

### Cited Findings
**Frameworks**
- NIST IR 8401, "Satellite Ground Segment: Applying the Cybersecurity Framework to Satellite Command and Control" (final 3 Jan 2023). It is a CSF profile covering terminals, Mission Operations Centers and Payload Operations Centers, written in support of SPD-5. NIST focuses on the ground because on-board controls are limited by SWaP. — [NIST CSRC](https://csrc.nist.gov/news/2023/nist-releases-nist-ir-8401); [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/nist-finalizes-cybersecurity-guidance-ground-segment-space-operations/); [Industrial Cyber](https://industrialcyber.co/nist/nist-rolls-out-cybersecurity-profile-that-focuses-on-command-and-control-of-satellite-buses-payloads/). Note: it is based on CSF 1.1, and I found no CSF 2.0 update of IR 8401 as of Oct 2026.
- NIST IR 8270, "Introduction to Cybersecurity for Commercial Satellite Operations" (final July 2023), applies the CSF to uncrewed commercial space vehicles. — [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2023/07/introduction-cybersecurity-commercial-satellite-operations-nist-ir-8270)
- CISA / SSCIWG "Recommendations to Space System Operators for Improving Cybersecurity" (Apr 2024) says the ground segment is "the most accessible and vulnerable part of the space infrastructure", and "securing ground segment technologies … remains one of the most effective ways to protect assets in space". — [CISA PDF](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-06/Recommendations%20to%20Space%20System%20Operators%20for%20Improving%20Cybersecurity%20(508).pdf)
- CISA's key point: once the C2 software is compromised, "an encrypted link … does not help … as the attacker can now carry out the attack using the system's legitimate encryption infrastructure". That makes ground-side controls plus a ground IDS critical. The IDS should flag "unauthorized maneuvers, malicious commands, or other anomalous behaviors … before the spacecraft is in view". — [CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-06/Recommendations%20to%20Space%20System%20Operators%20for%20Improving%20Cybersecurity%20(508).pdf)
- CISA's mapped controls are: segmentation (800-53 AC-4, AC-10, SC-7); supply chain (SR-2/3/4; SBOM/HBOM, pre-deployment testing, vendor self-attestation); least functionality (CM-7) to reject out-of-bounds or unhandled commands; incident monitoring IR-5; event logging AU-2; control assessments CA-2; continuous monitoring CA-7; encryption on links; and CRPA antennas against jamming. — [CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-06/Recommendations%20to%20Space%20System%20Operators%20for%20Improving%20Cybersecurity%20(508).pdf)

**SPARTA / Aerospace ground-segment DiD (most current and most detailed)**
- SPARTA v4.0 added ground-segment defense guidance and revised all 90 countermeasures across the lifecycle. — [Aerospace Medium](https://medium.com/the-aerospace-corporation/sparta-v4-0-breaking-new-ground-b2d3b7ab832f); [SPARTA countermeasures](https://sparta.aerospace.org/countermeasures/SPARTA)
- Aerospace report VTR-2026-00702 Rev A, "Ground Segment Cyber Defenses and Risk-Based Tiering" (6 Mar 2026), defines 8 DiD layers and 78 sub-layers: Data, Ground Software, Endpoint, Network, CND/Incident Response, Perimeter, Physical, Prevention. It uses a two-tier impact framework (Baseline vs Enhanced countermeasures) mapped to a functional decomposition of the ground segment, with a worked Viasat KA-SAT attack/defense case study. — [SPARTA PDF](https://sparta.aerospace.org/resources/VTR-2026-00702-Rev%20A%20-%20Ground%20System%20Cyber%20Defenses%20and%20Risk-Based%20Tiering.pdf)
- Ground Software layer sub-layers: build-environment configuration control, secure coding standards, CWE prevention, static and dynamic analysis, software SCRM, and threat modeling. Endpoint layer: application allow-listing, logging and auditing, separation of duties, file integrity, HIDS/HIPS, patching, least privilege, and vulnerability scanning. — [same](https://sparta.aerospace.org/resources/VTR-2026-00702-Rev%20A%20-%20Ground%20System%20Cyber%20Defenses%20and%20Risk-Based%20Tiering.pdf)
- Enhanced-tier crypto requirements: "All mission cryptographic material, including uplink keys, command authentication keys, signing certificates, and telemetry protection keys, shall be generated, stored, and managed exclusively within hardware security modules". FIPS-validated modules; TLS 1.3 with mutual authentication and certificate pinning; internal segments "shall not be assumed trusted". — [same](https://sparta.aerospace.org/resources/VTR-2026-00702-Rev%20A%20-%20Ground%20System%20Cyber%20Defenses%20and%20Risk-Based%20Tiering.pdf)
- Access control: ABAC with continuous validation and just-in-time privileged roles. "Dual-operator approval mechanisms or two-person control models shall be enforced for modification of command products, abort logic, cryptographic configurations, antenna pointing inputs". MFA is required for repositories holding command dictionaries, maneuver scripts, key stores, firmware and telemetry archives. Two-person approval is required to retrieve or modify mission keys and similar artifacts. — [same](https://sparta.aerospace.org/resources/VTR-2026-00702-Rev%20A%20-%20Ground%20System%20Cyber%20Defenses%20and%20Risk-Based%20Tiering.pdf)
- Audit: operator actions on HMIs must be logged with timestamp, identity, source system and affected control element, "sufficient context to reconstruct intent and system state". Logs must be tamper-protected, forwarded centrally, and also kept locally in limited form. — [same](https://sparta.aerospace.org/resources/VTR-2026-00702-Rev%20A%20-%20Ground%20System%20Cyber%20Defenses%20and%20Risk-Based%20Tiering.pdf)
- MFA "shall be required for privileged roles, mission operators" on engineering consoles. Where native MFA is impossible, use jump hosts. Use named accounts rather than shared ones. Use Zero Trust micro-segmentation between enterprise IT, mission ops and OT, with segmentation drift able to gate mission readiness. — [same](https://sparta.aerospace.org/resources/VTR-2026-00702-Rev%20A%20-%20Ground%20System%20Cyber%20Defenses%20and%20Risk-Based%20Tiering.pdf)

**Link security / key management**
- CCSDS 355.0-B-2 SDLS provides authentication, encryption and authenticated encryption for TC, TM and AOS frames. Typical use is AES-GCM, with sequence numbers for anti-replay. Supporting services are SA management, key management and monitoring and control. — [CCSDS 355.0-B-2](https://ccsds.org/Pubs/355x0b2.pdf); [CCSDS 350.11-G (SDLS report)](https://ccsds.org/Pubs/350x11g1e1.pdf)
- CCSDS 354.0-M-1 symmetric key management uses a master/session key hierarchy, with session keys rotated by over-the-air rekeying (OTAR). — [CCSDS 354.0-M-1](https://ccsds.org/Pubs/354x0m1.pdf); [ESA indico SDLS platform paper](https://indico.esa.int/event/528/attachments/5988/10191/Developing_a_CCSDS_compliant_platform_to_reliably_secure_current_and_future_space_data_links.pdf)

**Regulation / information sharing (2025–2026)**
- NIS2 already covers ground infrastructure operators that support space services. — [Mayer Brown, Dec 2025](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/securing-the-final-frontier-cybersecurity-risk-regulation-and-compliance-trends-in-space-and-satellite-operations)
- EU Space Act proposal (June 2025), Arts 75–95. Arts 80–82 protect "ground stations, command-and-control assets, telemetry systems and backup assets". Arts 83–85 cover authentication, secure configuration, encryption and key-management policy. Arts 86–87, 90 cover backup, redundancy, restoration and recovery. Art 88 covers TLPT. Art 92 covers supply chain. Art 93 sets reporting: early warning within 12h (Union assets) or 24h, a report within 72h, and a final report within one month. Status: in trilogue as of Mar 2026; proposed application date 1 Jan 2030 with transition to 2032. The Council text makes NIS2 the horizontal base rather than lex specialis. — [Hogan Lovells, 15 May 2026](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/the-eu-space-act-proposal-in-the-legislative-process-cybersecurity-market-access-and-implications); [E+H](https://www.eh.at/en/eu-space-act-vs-nis2-how-the-compromise-text-reshapes-the-cybersecurity-architecture/); [Cooley, Jul 2025](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-07-24-the-proposed-eu-space-act-10-key-implications-us-and-non-eu-satellite-operators-should-know)
- The Space ISAC Watch Center has five cells (Coordination, All-Source, Terrestrial, Signals, Space) covering ground, link, space, launch and user segments. Indicators are shared as STIX collections, and Phase II adds ground infrastructure security. — [Space ISAC](https://spaceisac.org/space-isac-operational-watch-center-enters-phase-ii-planning-to-keep-pace-with-proliferating-threats-to-space-systems/); [Space ISAC slick sheet](https://spaceisac.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Watch-Center-Slick-Sheet.pdf); [Via Satellite, Oct/Nov 2025](https://interactive.satellitetoday.com/via/october-november-2025/inside-the-space-isac-watch-center-keeping-an-eye-on-cyber-threats). OASIS SATIS TC (2024) is standardising automated space threat-intel sharing. — [OASIS SATIS charter](https://www.oasis-open.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Space-Automated-Threat-Intelligence-Sharing-SATIS-Charter.pdf)
- Market signal: OpenC3 sells SSO/SAML/RBAC, audit log and "FIPS-ready" only in COSMOS Enterprise, not in the open-source Core. Buyers treat these as paid "enterprise" features. — [OpenC3 pricing](https://openc3.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- For an MCS product, the minimum credible security feature set in 2026 is: OIDC/SAML SSO with MFA; RBAC that can grow into ABAC; per-command criticality with two-person (four-eyes) release for hazardous commands and for database or procedure changes; tamper-evident, append-only audit logs (hash-chained or WORM) exported to SIEM (syslog/OTel); SDLS support with HSM/PKCS#11 key handling and OTAR workflows; a command-anomaly detector (out-of-bounds or unscheduled commands); SBOM generation; and TLS/mTLS between services. These map directly to the SPARTA baseline tier and to CISA's 800-53 mappings.
- EU-facing customers will ask for incident-reporting support (evidence export, timelines) sized to 12h/24h/72h clocks.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether NIST has started a CSF 2.0 revision of IR 8401/8270.
- SPARTA's exact split of Baseline vs Enhanced for each of the 78 sub-layers is in Appendix B (about 70 pages) and was only sampled here.
- The ESA/ECSS security standard for ground segments (for example ECSS-Q-ST-80 or the ESA security directives) was not retrieved.

## Which HMI/alarm-management practices are recommended for control rooms?

### Takeaway
No space-specific alarm-management standard exists. ISA-18.2 / IEC 62682:2023 (the what) and EEMUA 191 (the how) are the reference practices, and EEMUA says they apply to other sectors "with appropriate interpretation". Their core is: alarm philosophy, rationalization, shelving/suppression, and KPIs. The KPIs are at most about 1 alarm per operator per 10 minutes in steady state, floods defined as more than 10 alarms in 10 minutes, and standing alarms kept to a minimum. Space-ops guidance adds multiple limit sets per mission phase, displays with units, states and limits validated against simulators, and the warning not to rely on the absence of alarms. ESA's EGS-CC experience shows UI customisability is the biggest user pain point.

### Cited Findings
- EEMUA 191 was developed with UK HSE input. ISA-18.2 and IEC 62682:2023 are aligned with it. The standards define what an alarm-management program contains, and EEMUA 191 adds how to do it. It targets process industries, but "much of the guidance is generic and with appropriate interpretation can be applied in other sectors". — [EEMUA 191](https://www.eemua.org/products/publications/digital/eemua-publication-191); [EEMUA news](https://www.eemua.org/news/good-practice-for-all-aspects-of-industrial-alarm-systems-%E2%80%93-new-edition-of-eemua-191-released); [IEC 62682](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEC_62682)
- KPIs: at most about 1 alarm per operator per 10 minutes in normal operation (about 150 per day), based on the Bransby and Jenkinson 1998 survey. A flood is more than 10 alarms in 10 minutes, and the percentage of time spent in flood is a primary health metric. A high standing-alarm count indicates poor design or maintenance. — [Alarm Load Scorer / EEMUA-ISA benchmarks](https://industrydigits.com/resources/alarm-load-scorer/); [Risktec](https://risktec.tuv.com/knowledge-bank/the-importance-of-assessing-alarm-performance/); [ProcessVue KPI paper](https://www.processvue.com/downloads/Alarm_system_performance_KPIs_V1_0.pdf). These are secondary or vendor sources, and the EEMUA text itself is paywalled.
- Rationalization: each alarm needs a defined cause, consequence, operator action and priority. This is the core of ISA-18.2. — [exida](https://www.exida.com/Alarm-Management/Detail/standards_guidelines); [Seqent explainer](https://seqent.com/blog/alarm-rationalization-explained/)
- Space operations: the TM database "may contain multiple sets of limits to account for different operating environments" (launch, transfer, on-orbit). The HMI "should display all telemetry accurately with proper units or states and limits", verified by SMEs and a simulator. Trend deviations approaching critical out-of-limit values should be watch-listed. — [Aerospace TOR-2013-00293](https://aerospace.org/sites/default/files/maiw/TOR-2013-00293.pdf)
- "The absence of alarms does not mean that the other units or subsystems were not affected by the anomaly". Plot all subsystems around the anomaly window. — [same](https://aerospace.org/sites/default/files/maiw/TOR-2013-00293.pdf)
- ESOC's EGS-CC migration: "a significant part of issues … concentrated around user interface issues". ESA responded with "more customization capabilities of the user interface … while still keeping parts common". — [SpaceOps 2025 #477, Towards EGS-CC at ESOC](https://publications.spaceops.org/2025/download_by_id.php?id=0477)
- Constellation operations favour integrated, contextual timelines, where the operator moves from a timeline event to the related TM or command history in one interface, with operator-in-the-loop escalation and progressive automation. — [Terma blog (undated, vendor)](https://blog.terma.com/how-to-ground-control-should-handle-satellite-constellations)
- Historic NASA work: guidelines for the human-factors design of spacecraft ground control rooms (C. M. Mitchell, GSFC), and auditory alarm design for Emergency/Warning/Caution classes per ISO 7731 (older). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23882411_Human_factors_aspects_of_control_room_design_Guidelines_and_annotated_bibliography); [Auditory alarm design for CEV](https://www.academia.edu/57581871/Auditory_Alarm_Design_for_Nasa_Cev_Applications?uc-sb-sw=5482335)
- ESA's classic MCS (SCOS-2000) display types are alphanumeric (AND), graphical/plot (GRD) and mimic/synoptic (MMD). — [ESA Bulletin 108, SCOS-2000](https://www.esa.int/esapub/bulletin/bullet108/chapter8_bul108.pdf) (older; I did not re-verify the display types in this fetch).

### Inferences
- MCS gaps to check: an alarm philosophy document; per-parameter priority, consequence and action metadata; shelving with expiry and audit; phase- or context-dependent limit sets; flood suppression and first-out; alarm KPI dashboards (rate per 10 min, flood %, top-10 bad actors, standing alarms); audible alarm classes; and user-configurable displays built from shared common widgets.

### Gaps
- I found no ESA or NASA document that formally adopts ISA-18.2 or EEMUA 191 for spacecraft operations. Their application to space is an inference.
- The text of the ECSS-E-ST-70-31 display requirements was not retrieved.

## What cross-cutting platform capabilities (HA/redundancy, archive and reporting, simulators and AIT reuse, DevOps/cloud, licensing/cost) are expected?

### Takeaway
Expected capabilities are: hot-standby MCS servers with TM/TC context synchronisation; geo-diverse prime and backup control centres for commercial GEO and constellation operators; a two-tier archive (short-term and long-term) holding frames, packets, parameters, events, logs, alarms and command history; routine trend reports; operational simulators based on SMP2 models, reused from AIT/SVF to training; one MCS core from AIV to operations (EGS-CC); Kubernetes/Helm deployment in newer products; and licensing that ranges from free (SCOS-2000 for the European community; Yamcs and COSMOS Core open source) to about $33k–$165k per year (COSMOS Enterprise).

### Cited Findings
**High availability / redundancy**
- ESOC MCS heritage: ENVISAT validated "hot-standby system reconfiguration and machines and data resynchronisation following failures for all mission phases", with TM and TC context saved on the redundant server for smooth switchover. — [ADS abstract, SMART-1 reuse, 2002 (older)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ESASP.509E..62P/abstract)
- SES runs "two geographically diverse satellite flight operations centers manned 24/7/365 with redundant and diverse data centers". — [SES](https://www.ses.com/solutions/space-services/satellite-flight-operations-and-engineering)
- For constellations, "no single data repository should represent a point of failure". — [Terma blog](https://blog.terma.com/how-to-ground-control-should-handle-satellite-constellations)
- The EU Space Act proposal makes "backup, redundancy, restoration and recovery measures" a legal obligation (Arts 86–87, 90). — [Hogan Lovells 2026](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/the-eu-space-act-proposal-in-the-legislative-process-cybersecurity-market-access-and-implications)
- AWS Ground Station SLA: 99.9% monthly uptime per region, with service credits of 10%, 25% and 100% at under 99.9%, 99% and 95%. This is a useful benchmark for availability SLAs on GSaaS-integrated MCS. — [AWS Ground Station SLA](https://aws.amazon.com/ground-station/sla)

**Archive / trending / reporting**
- ESOC's EGS-CC archive must store "telemetry frames, packets, parameters, events, log messages, alarm states and control activities", handle performance peaks and many filter combinations, and minimise the total cost of ownership of the data. — [SpaceOps 2025 #477](https://publications.spaceops.org/2025/download_by_id.php?id=0477)
- Two-tier archive: a Short-Term Archive (recent, high-rate data) and a Long-Term Archive (full mission history). — [Terma](https://blog.terma.com/how-to-ground-control-should-handle-satellite-constellations)
- Trending: "a standard set of trending reports are identified … and reviewed at regular intervals". Ground tools should generate routine trend reports. Tools fall into two classes, collection/monitoring and predictive analysis. — [Aerospace TOR-2013-00293](https://aerospace.org/sites/default/files/maiw/TOR-2013-00293.pdf)
- COSMOS 7 introduced a new time-series database, and "distributed, long-retention logging" is Enterprise-only. — [OpenC3 COSMOS 7 launch](https://openc3.com/news/cosmos-7-launch); [OpenC3 pricing](https://openc3.com/pricing)

**Simulators / AIT-to-operations reuse**
- ESA operational spacecraft simulators "are used for testing of ground data systems, validation of procedures and training of the flight control team". SIMSAT is the ESOC simulation runtime (GUI, model scheduling, event and fault logging, model data visualisation). SMP2 (initiated in 1999, released in 2005) gives model portability across simulators and missions. — [ESA ISIS/SMP2 paper](https://indico.esa.int/event/180/contributions/1353/attachments/1253/1478/1400-Pham_Paper.pdf); [ESOC software products](https://esoc.esa.int/services-software); [Swarm constellation simulator, SpaceOps 2010](https://arc.aiaa.org/doi/pdf/10.2514/6.2010-2323)
- The same models are reused across the SVF/AIT simulators (spacecraft prime) and the operations training simulator (TOMS at CNES), even when the runtime environments differ. — [search summary of CNES/ESA model reuse paper](https://indico.esa.int/event/108/contributions/147/attachments/203/235/05_03_Sebastiao_paper.pdf). I did not verify this against the full text.
- EGS-CC: ESOC reconfirmed in Apr 2024 that its next MCS will be based on EGS-CC. It first enters operations in 2026 (Swarm pilot), with a fully operational solution in 2027 and Ariel (launch 2029) as the first native mission. SCOS-2000 end of life is no earlier than 2033, across more than 40 missions. "Category 3" missions receive tailoring data, including flight control procedures, directly in EGS-CC TDM format from industry, "normally … the case for missions using EGS-CC as well as AIV system". This is the "same software from AIT to operations" model. — [SpaceOps 2025 #477](https://publications.spaceops.org/2025/download_by_id.php?id=0477)
- E-70 itself notes that operations must "manage test configurations to support both space/ground system integration and flight operations". — [ECSS E-70 training](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/ECSS-E-70-Training-Presentation-v1-(2021).pdf)

**DevOps / cloud-native**
- OpenC3 COSMOS runs on Kubernetes via Helm and scales to constellations. EOEPCA compiles CWL into Argo workflows. — [arXiv 2607.14798 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14798v1); [OpenC3 reference architectures](https://docs.openc3.com/docs/guides/reference-architectures)
- Ground segments in 2025–2030 are moving to software-defined, cloud, virtualised and SDN infrastructure. — [TS2 (secondary, low authority)](https://ts2.tech/en/ground-control-goes-cloud-the-digital-overhaul-of-satellite-operations-2025-2030/)
- EGS-CC challenges include building a new archive on modern IT and the process overhead of working in a community. — [SpaceOps 2025 #477](https://publications.spaceops.org/2025/download_by_id.php?id=0477)

**Licensing / cost benchmarks**

### Inferences
- For an MCS competing in 2026, table stakes are: an active/standby or active/active backend with a replicated archive and command-context failover; a documented RPO/RTO; a time-series archive with tiered retention; scheduled trend and pass reports; SMP2/FMI or simple simulator interfaces so procedures can be validated before flight; container and Helm deployment; and OpenTelemetry/Prometheus observability. Security and audit features are the main thing vendors use to separate paid tiers from free ones.

### Gaps
- I found no public price lists for Kratos EPOCH/OS COMET, L3Harris InControl, Terma TGSS or GMV hifly. Proprietary MCS pricing is quote-only.
- I found no authoritative public source for standard pass report or monthly report contents, or for data-retention durations (for example "mission life + N years"). These are typically contract- or agency-specific (ESA long-term data preservation policy was not retrieved).
