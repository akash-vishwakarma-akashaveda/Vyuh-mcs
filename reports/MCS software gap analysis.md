# What VYUH-MCS must build before it flies

VYUH-MCS already handles the parts of mission control that a live demo exercises: CCSDS 132.0 frames and 133.0 packets, a complete COP-1 FOP-1, a ground safety chain, two-person command approval, a reviewed mission-database release workflow and a modern web console. It is missing the parts an operations readiness review exercises. There is **no persistent archive**, **no ground-station interface (SLE or GSaaS API)**, **only PUS service 1**, **no CFDP or memory load/dump**, **link encryption that is not CCSDS SDLS**, **no real identity provider or server-side enforcement of roles and approvals**, **no redundancy**, **no procedure language**, and **no TLE, OEM or CDM handling**. Each of these appears as baseline in at least two of the surveyed products. Yamcs, the closest open-source benchmark, ships SDLS, CFDP, time correlation, replication and a timeline at no licence cost ([Yamcs services](https://docs.yamcs.org/yamcs-server-manual/services/)). OpenC3 sells RBAC, SSO, CFDP and CCSDS framing as a $33k–$165k/yr enterprise tier ([OpenC3 Enterprise](https://openc3.com/enterprise/)). Terma CCS5 bundles PUS-A/C, CFDP, SLE, SDLS with key-management UIs and an SQL archive ([Terma CCS5/TSC 2024](https://tgss-static.terma.com/public/PresentationCcs5andTsc.pdf)). Basic TM/TC is therefore a commodity, and VYUH cannot charge for it. The gap list below has about 35 must-have items. Roughly a third are infrastructure (archive, HA, identity) that every other gap depends on, so the roadmap starts there rather than with protocol features. VYUH's real differentiators are an ML anomaly detector inside the MCS, a tenant-isolated customer portal and a reviewed database release workflow. They will count only once the flight-blocking floor is in place.

## The 2026 baseline is set by open source, not by legacy vendors

Every serious product now covers the same core: database-driven CCSDS TM/TC, per-command verification, limits and alarms, an archive with replay and trending, a procedure language, a scheduler, ground-equipment monitoring and control, a training simulator, redundancy and an external API. Kratos EPOCH has offered this since its GEO heritage: CCSDS TM/TC with encryption, memory management, archive and playback, STOL automation, an open API, and redundancy by running multiple copies ([EPOCH T&C datasheet, legacy](https://www.kratosspace.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/EPOCH-TC-Server.pdf)). L3Harris InControl adds onboard system management and a simulator for training and procedure development ([L3Harris](https://www.l3harris.com/all-capabilities/incontrol-satellite-command-and-control)). What changed between 2024 and 2026 is that this baseline became free. Yamcs 5.12.2 added SDLS with AES-256-GCM, 5.13.0 added activity chaining on the timeline, and 5.13.6 added PUS service 22 together with security hardening ([Yamcs release notes](https://docs.yamcs.org/yamcs-relnotes/changelog/)). ESA's EGOS-CC is "freely available to European entities under ESA Community licence" ([ESOC](https://esoc.esa.int/content/egos-cc-commanding-satellites-shadows)).

The commercial vendors have moved up a layer as a result. Their selling points are fleet dashboards (Terma AutoPilot, GMV FleetDashboard), Kubernetes scale-out, SaaS delivery (Telespazio EASE), built-in GSaaS adapters, AI planning (Cognitive Space) and support for software-defined satellites. Kratos EPOCH passed factory acceptance with Airbus OneSat in December 2025 ([SatNews](https://satnews.com/2025/12/30/kratos-epoch-c2-software-completes-factory-acceptance-testing-for-airbus-onesat-platform/)). Telesat put GMV's orchestrator, FleetDashboard and Archiva analytics on top of hifly for 198 LEO satellites ([GMV](https://www.gmv.com/en/communication/press-room/press-releases/telecommunications/gmv-deliver-next-generation-satellite)). Flight dynamics and planning remain separate products that plug into the MCS: FocusSuite and Flexplan at GMV, OASYS at Kratos, STK/ODTK, FreeFlyer, and Orekit or GMAT for teams building their own ([GMV FocusSuite](https://www.gmv.com/en/products/space/focussuite), [Kratos OASYS](https://www.kratosspace.com/products/satellites/command-and-control/oasys), [Orekit](https://www.orekit.org/site-orekit-13.0.3/architecture/estimation.html)).

**Priority key used below.** *Must* means VYUH cannot pass an operations readiness review or support a real spacecraft without it. *Should* means buyers expect it and its absence loses deals or adds operator workload. *Differentiator* means a minority of competitors have it and it would set VYUH apart. All priorities assume a LEO EO or smallsat constellation customer using PUS-C. Agency customers move several Should items up to Must.

## Space link, ground network and on-board services: the flight-blocking core

### TM/TC and link layer

VYUH's frame and packet chain covers the core standards (132.0, 133.0, CLCW to FOP-1). The gaps are at the edges of the link. CCSDS lists 131.0-B-5, 132.0-B-3, 232.0-B-4, 732.0-B-4 AOS, 732.1-B-3 USLP and 301.0-B-4 time codes as the current issues ([CCSDS Pubs](https://ccsds.org/Pubs/132x0b3.pdf)). Yamcs supports AOS, TM and USLP frames with no, CRC16 or CRC32 error control, and wraps TC frames into CLTUs with BCH or LDPC plus randomisation ([Yamcs frames](https://docs.yamcs.org/yamcs-server-manual/links/ccsds-frame-processing/)). The SLE FCLTU service carries CLTUs, so if the station does not encode them, the MCS must.

### Ground-station network integration

This is the most consequential gap. Without it VYUH cannot receive telemetry from a real antenna. Agency and commercial networks expose **SLE RAF/RCF/ROCF/FCLTU** as the standard MCS interface, implemented by the Yamcs SLE plugin and ESA's open-source `sleapi-j` ([Yamcs SLE](https://docs.yamcs.org/yamcs-sle/about/), [esa/sleapi-j](https://github.com/esa/sleapi-j)). New-space networks sell "single API" access instead. ATLAS Freedom offers a REST Flex Scheduler "for client lights-out operations" across 50+ antennas ([ATLAS](https://atlasspace.com/freedom-gsaas/)). Leaf Line exposes one API plus a real-time data interface ([Leaf](https://leaf.space/leaf-line/)). KSAT is integrating AWS Ground Station into its offering ([Via Satellite](https://www.satellitetoday.com/technology/2025/07/30/ksat-to-integrate-aws-ground-station-capabilities-into-its-offerings/)). Microsoft retired Azure Orbital Ground Station in December 2024 ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/microsoft-retires-ground-station-services-sells-antenna-to-sli/)), so adapters belong behind a provider abstraction.

### PUS services

PUS-C (ECSS-E-ST-70-41C, 2016) remains the active edition. The standard "assumes that the mission ground segment … implements all standardized capabilities" ([ECSS-E-ST-70-41C Rev.1 DIR1](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/ECSS-E-ST-70-41C-Rev.1-DIR1(11December2024).pdf)). The Rev.1 draft (public review Dec 2024–Feb 2025) removes ST[8] and adds CFDP-based ST[24]–[27]. It was not confirmed as published by Oct 2026. VYUH handles only ST[1] acceptance and completion. The standards research identifies **ST[1, 3, 5, 9, 11, 15, 17, 20] as the LEO EO core** and ST[6, 12, 13, 19, 23] as commonly tailored in. That list is an inference from service semantics; no survey of operator usage exists. Terma advertises PUS-A and PUS-C with "PUS not mandatory" ([Terma](https://tgss-static.terma.com/public/PresentationCcs5andTsc.pdf)). Pixxel had to fork Yamcs to make ST[11] scheduling configurable ([pixxelhq/yamcs PR #229](https://github.com/pixxelhq/yamcs/pull/229)). Tailorable PUS support is still therefore a competitive weak spot across the field.

### File transfer and on-board software management

PUS Rev.1 adopts CFDP for all file transactions between space and ground ([Rev.1 DIR1 §6.24](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/ECSS-E-ST-70-41C-Rev.1-DIR1(11December2024).pdf)). That makes **CFDP Class 2 a de-facto requirement for new ECSS missions**, and it is how payload products and software images move on modern smallsats. Yamcs ships CFDP, Terma ships it, OpenC3 sells it in Enterprise, and AIT reaches it through AIT-DSN. ESOC treats on-board software maintenance (memory load/dump) as a next-tier EGS-CC requirement ([SpaceOps 2025 #477](https://publications.spaceops.org/2025/download_by_id.php?id=0477)). Kratos lists memory management in EPOCH's baseline ([EPOCH datasheet](https://www.kratosspace.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/EPOCH-TC-Server.pdf)).

## Data, commanding and the operator: correct in the console, missing on the server

### Mission database

VYUH's XTCE-like JSON dictionaries and two-reviewer release workflow are a good start. They are, however, a closed format. **XTCE (CCSDS 660.0-B-2) is the common interchange format and Yamcs's native model** ([660.0-B-2](https://ccsds.org/Pubs/660x0b2.pdf), [Yamcs MDB](https://docs.yamcs.org/yamcs-server-manual/mdb/)). European spacecraft primes deliver SCOS-2000 MIB tables. Terma kept the MIB as its data model (MIB 6.9–7.2) and EGS-CC ships a MIB-to-TDM converter ([Terma](https://tgss-static.terma.com/public/PresentationCcs5andTsc.pdf), [SpaceOps 2025 #477](https://publications.spaceops.org/2025/download_by_id.php?id=0477)). Without import, every customer onboarding begins with hand transcription, and ESOC warns that moving to a new data model "must not be underestimated".

| Derived/synthetic parameter engine (expressions or scripts over other parameters) | Must | OpenC3 (DERIVED + Ruby/Python conversions), Terma (several synthetic languages), Yamcs (algorithms) | [OpenC3 telemetry](https://docs.openc3.com/docs/configuration/telemetry), [Terma](https://tgss-static.terma.com/public/PresentationCcs5andTsc.pdf) |

### Commanding and verification

VYUH's ground safety chain and two-person approval match what ESA calls pre-release command checks. Verification, though, only matches PUS-1 acceptance and completion reports. SCOS-2000's advantage is **per-command criteria and time windows that account for propagation delay and jitter**, rather than a single rule for every command ([ESAW Armitage](https://indico.esa.int/event/93/contributions/3576/attachments/2798/3255/1530_Armitage.pdf)). Two-person approval also runs in the console. CISA's central warning applies directly here: once C2 software is compromised, "an encrypted link … does not help" ([CISA SSCIWG](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-06/Recommendations%20to%20Space%20System%20Operators%20for%20Improving%20Cybersecurity%20(508).pdf)). Approval therefore has to be enforced by the backend.

|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-stage verification (released, radiated, received, accepted, started, progress, completed) with per-command timeouts | Must | SCOS-2000, Yamcs | [ESAW Armitage](https://indico.esa.int/event/93/contributions/3576/attachments/2798/3255/1530_Armitage.pdf) |
| Server-side enforcement of two-person approval and command authorisation (not console-only) | Must | SPARTA baseline requirement | [SPARTA VTR-2026-00702](https://sparta.aerospace.org/resources/VTR-2026-00702-Rev%20A%20-%20Ground%20System%20Cyber%20Defenses%20and%20Risk-Based%20Tiering.pdf) |
| Persistent command history (part of the archive gap) | Must | All surveyed MCSs; EGS-CC archive includes control activities | [SpaceOps 2025 #477](https://publications.spaceops.org/2025/download_by_id.php?id=0477) |
| Command stacks mixing commands, comments and verification steps; time-tagged command uplink | Must | Yamcs 5.9.9, Terma ("tested TC stacks") | [Yamcs release notes](https://docs.yamcs.org/yamcs-relnotes/changelog/), [Terma](https://tgss-static.terma.com/public/PresentationCcs5andTsc.pdf) |

### Archive, replay and reporting

This is VYUH's largest single deficiency, and most other features depend on it. VYUH has no archive: the Postgres/TimescaleDB migrations are not wired in, Kafka and Redis are in-memory substitutes, and the console's parameter history is synthetic. ESOC's requirement for the EGS-CC archive is that it store "telemetry frames, packets, parameters, events, log messages, alarm states and control activities", survive performance peaks and support many filter combinations ([SpaceOps 2025 #477](https://publications.spaceops.org/2025/download_by_id.php?id=0477)). Terma archives to MySQL, MariaDB, PostgreSQL or Aurora in plain SQL, stores TM on change, keeps a long-term compressed archive and offers retrieval tools that run without CCS ([Terma](https://tgss-static.terma.com/public/PresentationCcs5andTsc.pdf)). Yamcs 5.11 unified archive, replay and cache into one Parameter Retrieval Service ([Yamcs release notes](https://docs.yamcs.org/yamcs-relnotes/changelog/)). Trending also matters as an operational process: a "standard set of trending reports" is reviewed at regular intervals ([Aerospace TOR-2013-00293](https://aerospace.org/sites/default/files/maiw/TOR-2013-00293.pdf)).

### Alarms and HMI

The alarm manager's core logic (dedup, a single open alarm per parameter, escalation, clear on return to normal) is sound. Shelving is the exception: it is partly console-local, so a browser refresh or a second operator does not see it. There is no space-specific alarm standard. ISA-18.2 / IEC 62682 and EEMUA 191 are the reference practice. Their KPIs are **about 1 alarm per operator per 10 minutes** in steady state, with a flood defined as **more than 10 alarms in 10 minutes** ([EEMUA 191](https://www.eemua.org/products/publications/digital/eemua-publication-191), [ProcessVue KPI paper](https://www.processvue.com/downloads/Alarm_system_performance_KPIs_V1_0.pdf)). These secondary benchmarks are an inference for space use; no agency formally adopts them. On HMI, ESOC found that "a significant part of issues" in the EGS-CC migration were UI issues, and answered with more customisation ([SpaceOps 2025 #477](https://publications.spaceops.org/2025/download_by_id.php?id=0477)). VYUH's redesign is aimed at exactly the weakness where incumbents are exposed.

## Orbits, plans and fleets: delegate the physics, own the interfaces

### Flight dynamics and collision avoidance

VYUH computes contacts from a simplified two-body + J2 model with no TLE ingest, and its conjunction display uses a demo catalogue. The research supports a clear split of responsibilities. **The MCS owns TLE/OMM ingest, SGP4-level pass prediction, OEM import/export, CDM ingest and display, and scheduling. It delegates precision OD, manoeuvre design and Pc computation** to FocusSuite, ODTK, FreeFlyer, OASYS, Orekit or GMAT. Orekit (Apache 2.0) covers SGP4, batch least squares and Kalman OD, and reads and writes OPM, OEM, OMM and OCM ([Orekit](https://www.orekit.org/site-orekit-13.0.3/architecture/estimation.html)), which makes it the natural sidecar for a Go backend. On the regulatory side, the FCC's 5-year LEO disposal rule applies to new applications from 29 Sep 2024 ([FCC 22-74](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-22-74A1.pdf)). ESA's Oct 2025 requirements set a **1e-4 action threshold** and require recurrent manoeuvre capability for constellations ([ESA](https://blogs.esa.int/spacesafety-community/files/2026/02/ESAs-Internal-Zero-Debris-Policy-and-Space-Debris-Mitigation-Requirements.pdf)). TraCSS distributes CDMs per CCSDS 508.0, asks operators for OCM ephemerides and offers on-demand manoeuvre screening, but is still in "pilot" pending FY27 funding, so Space-Track stays the system of record ([Via Satellite](https://www.satellitetoday.com/government-military/2026/08/26/tracss-traffic-coordination-system-remains-in-pilot-mode-due-to-budget-uncertainty/), [TraCSS CDM spec](https://space.commerce.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/TraCSS-Spec-001-v2.1_CDM.pdf)). Starlink manoeuvres autonomously at Pc above 3e-7, more than 40 times per satellite per year ([Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/satellites/every-spacex-starlink-satellite-has-to-dodge-a-collision-almost-weekly-and-experts-fear-the-worst)). Action thresholds must therefore be configurable across three orders of magnitude.

## Platform, security and business model: where readiness reviews and audits fail

### Security and key management

VYUH's custom AES-256-GCM with sequence numbers uses the same primitives as SDLS but cannot interoperate with an SDLS-equipped spacecraft. **CCSDS 355.0-B-2** defines security headers and trailers, per-virtual-channel Security Associations and anti-replay. **355.1-B-1** adds OTAR, key activation and SA management ([355.0-B-2](https://ccsds.org/Pubs/355x0b2.pdf), [355.1-B-1](https://ccsds.org/Pubs/355x1b1.pdf)). Aerospace's March 2026 ground-segment tiering requires MFA for mission operators, named accounts, two-person control over command products and crypto configuration, and, at the enhanced tier, all mission keys held "exclusively within hardware security modules" ([SPARTA VTR-2026-00702](https://sparta.aerospace.org/resources/VTR-2026-00702-Rev%20A%20-%20Ground%20System%20Cyber%20Defenses%20and%20Risk-Based%20Tiering.pdf)). No regulator mandates SDLS specifically. It arrives as an agency or customer requirement.

### Licensing

The market has three price points. Agency software is free to Europeans (SCOS-2000, EGOS-CC). Open-core runs from free to **$33k–$165k/yr** with no seat limits (OpenC3), with Yamcs under AGPL plus a commercial licence. Terma sells a **perpetual licence plus 20%/yr** ([OpenC3 pricing](https://openc3.com/pricing), [Yamcs Enterprise](https://yamcs.org/enterprise/), [Terma](https://tgss-static.terma.com/public/PresentationCcs5andTsc.pdf)). Kratos, L3Harris and GMV quote privately. VYUH has no published licence model. The open opportunity is the reverse of OpenC3's paywall: include SSO, RBAC, audit and SDLS in the base tier, because buyers already treat them as essential. Priority: Should (commercial readiness), Differentiator if priced transparently.

## VYUH already matches or beats the field in nine places

On the link, VYUH's **COP-1 implementation** (K=10 window, T1 retransmission, lockout recovery, Unlock and Set V(R)) covers the FOP-1 duties the standard requires ([dariol83/ccsds](https://github.com/dariol83/ccsds/blob/master/README.md)) and matches Yamcs's AD and BC handling. Its **TM chain** (fixed-length frames, ASM sync, CRC-16 FECF, packet reassembly across frames, per-VC dedup, reorder and gap detection, CLCW extraction) is the 132.0/133.0 core at par. On the command path, the **ground safety chain** (argument ranges, inhibits, interlocks) is the "pre-release command checks" ESOC prioritised for EGS-CC ([SpaceOps 2025 #477](https://publications.spaceops.org/2025/download_by_id.php?id=0477)). **Role-based two-person approval** is a SPARTA requirement that COSMOS Core does not offer at all ([SPARTA](https://sparta.aerospace.org/resources/VTR-2026-00702-Rev%20A%20-%20Ground%20System%20Cyber%20Defenses%20and%20Risk-Based%20Tiering.pdf)), though it has to move server-side to count.

The **database release workflow** (two reviewers, simulator check, rollback) is ahead of most products on paper. It is close to what ECSS E-70 asks for in configuration control ([ECSS E-70](https://ecss.nl/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/ECSS-E-70-Training-Presentation-v1-(2021).pdf)) and gives VYUH something concrete to show at an ORR. The **TM processor** (calibration, limits with hysteresis, static and measured OBT→UTC correlation, time-jump checks) is at par. The **web-native realtime gateway** with conflation and resume delivers the "installation-free" browser access ESOC lists as an EGS-CC strength.

Three VYUH features are differentiators. The first is **unsupervised anomaly detection inside the MCS**: no incumbent publicly documents ML anomaly detection in hifly, EPOCH or InControl, where anomaly handling is rule- or trigger-based ([Kratos EPOCH](https://www.kratosspace.com/products/satellites/command-and-control/epoch-ips), [Parsons](https://www.parsons.com/products/ace-ctrlpoint/)). An F1 of 0.42 on OPS-SAT-AD is a modest score, so VYUH should sell it as triage assistance, not detection of record. The second is the **tenant-isolated customer portal with payload delivery tracking**, which only Spire's productised platform and Capella's in-house flow resemble ([Spire CMP](https://spire.com/space-services/constellation-management-platform/), [AWS/Capella](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/publicsector/capella-uses-space-bring-you-closer-earth)). The third is the **12-spacecraft simulator with OPS-SAT flight replay and link impairment**, together with gap replay, a dead-letter monitor and Prometheus counters, which give operators fault visibility that COSMOS keeps in its paid tier. The audit hash chain and the Cesium globe point the right way. The first has to move to the backend; the second only becomes useful with real ephemerides.

## Prioritised roadmap: foundation first, then the flight path, then the edge

The order follows dependencies, not feature appeal. CFDP, playback merge, trending and audit all need an archive. Hot standby needs persisted COP-1 state. Server-side approval needs a real identity. Building PUS services first would mean building them twice.

|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Make it real (foundation)** | Nothing in memory that must survive a restart | Wire TimescaleDB archive (frames, packets, parameters, events, alarms, command history, audit) and retrieval API, replacing synthetic history; replace in-memory Kafka/Redis; OIDC/SAML SSO + MFA, named accounts; backend-enforced RBAC, two-person approval, shelving and hash-chained audit with SIEM export; mTLS between services; active/standby with persisted FOP-1 state | Must |
| **1. Two caveats affect sequencing. The PUS core list is an inference from service semantics; no operator usage survey exists. The first customer's PUS tailoring and ICD should therefore override the Phase 2 list. Also, if the first customer is a single new-space spacecraft on a GSaaS network, SLE can move behind the GSaaS adapter, because those networks expose REST and streaming APIs rather than SLE.

## Conclusion

The gap analysis shows a pattern: VYUH's missing pieces are not exotic. They are the things that only appear when a system has to survive a restart, a second operator, an auditor and a real antenna. That is good news for scope, because about half of the must-haves have well-trodden open implementations VYUH can wrap rather than write: ESA's `sleapi-j` for SLE, Orekit for SGP4, OEM, OCM and OD, and the CCSDS reference stacks for CFDP and SDLS. It is bad news for positioning, because once those pieces are in place VYUH is at par with a free Yamcs, so they buy eligibility, not differentiation.

The defensible position is therefore above the protocol layer. The incumbents' acknowledged weak points are UI usability (ESOC's main EGS-CC complaint), paywalled security (OpenC3), self-integrated PUS tailoring (Pixxel's Yamcs fork), and rule-only anomaly handling (Kratos, Parsons). VYUH's console redesign, built-in approval and audit, anomaly detection and customer portal each sit on one of those weak points. The roadmap's real risk is spending the redesign effort before Phase 0. A polished console over synthetic history and in-memory queues demos well and fails its first readiness review.
